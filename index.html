Climate & Population Health

Research on the demographic consequences of heat exposure and climate variability, with a focus on maternal and perinatal health outcomes in Ghana. Dissertation work links ERA5-HEAT reanalysis data with the Demographic and Health Surveys (2003–2022).

Health Inequalities

About my research

Population health, climate, and demographic inequality

I am a doctoral candidate in Demography and Sociology at the University of Pennsylvania, with concurrent training in Statistics and Data Science. My research examines how environmental exposures, geographic context, and social inequality jointly shape population health outcomes — particularly maternal, perinatal, and child health in sub-Saharan Africa.

My dissertation links ERA5-HEAT reanalysis data with the Ghana Demographic and Health Surveys (2003–2022) to estimate the effects of extreme heat exposure on pregnancy outcomes and infant survival. The analytical framework draws on Bayesian spatiotemporal modelling, distributed lag non-linear models, and target-trial emulation. Concurrently, I work as a Research Data Scientist on the USAID HEARTH project at Penn, supporting multi-country data integration on health and environment linkages.

Before joining Penn, I spent seven years in the Ghanaian public sector with the Ghana Health Service and the Ghana Statistical Service, including service as a District Census Officer for the 2021 Population and Housing Census. That experience grounds my methodological work in the practical realities of population data collection and policy use.

I am committed to reproducible, open scientific practice. Where data licences permit, replication code accompanies my publications, and methodological tools developed in the course of my research are released as documented repositories.
